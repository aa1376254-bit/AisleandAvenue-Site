What to Expect at a Bridal Makeup Trial

Last reviewed October 2026 · Trials

What a trial is for

A trial lets you test the look, the products and the artist's working style before the wedding. It also shows how the makeup photographs and how it lasts through a few hours of wear.

When to book it

Most brides schedule the trial a few months before the wedding, early enough to change your mind but close enough that your skin and hair will be similar to the wedding day. Book it on a day you have photos or events planned, so you can see how the look holds up.

What to bring

  • Two or three photos of looks you like, and one of looks you don't
  • A picture of your dress neckline and any jewelry
  • Your hair plan, if the same day includes a hair trial
  • A clean, moisturized face, as you would wear on the day

Questions to ask

  • Which products will you use for my skin type?
  • How long will the look last, and what do you pack for touch-ups?
  • How much time do you need for me and for my bridal party?
  • Do you have a backup artist if you are unavailable?

How to give feedback

Be specific. "Slightly less blush on the cheeks" helps more than "it's not quite right." Take photos in daylight and with flash, and look at them an hour later. If you have allergies or sensitive skin, tell the artist before the trial starts.

If you have a skin concern, speak with a qualified professional before trying new products close to the wedding.
